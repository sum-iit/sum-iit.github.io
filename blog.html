The Polyak–Łojasiewicz Condition: A Gentle Introduction

Optimization Online Learning

The PL condition is a relaxation of strong convexity that still enables linear convergence of gradient descent on non-convex functions. A function $f$ satisfies PL with parameter $\mu > 0$ if $\frac{1}{2}\|\nabla f(x)\|^2 \geq \mu(f(x) - f^*)$ for all $x$. We unpack what this means geometrically and why it is the right tool for analyzing overparameterized models.

Why Does FedAvg Converge? An Intuitive Walkthrough

Federated Learning Optimization

FedAvg is the workhorse of federated learning, yet its convergence under data heterogeneity is subtle. The core issue is client drift: local SGD steps pull each client's iterate toward its own minimizer $x_i^* = \arg\min F_i(x)$, away from the global minimizer $x^* = \arg\min \frac{1}{n}\sum_i F_i(x)$. Understanding this drift is key to both the algorithm's failure modes and its fixes.

Dynamic Regret vs. Static Regret: What's the Difference and Why It Matters

Online Learning

Static regret measures performance against the best fixed action in hindsight: $\mathrm{Regret}_T = \sum_{t=1}^T \ell_t(x_t) - \min_x \sum_{t=1}^T \ell_t(x)$. When the environment is non-stationary, the optimal action itself shifts over time and dynamic regret $\mathrm{D\text{-}Regret}_T = \sum_{t=1}^T \bigl[\ell_t(x_t) - \ell_t(x_t^*)\bigr]$ is the right measure. We explore when sub-linear dynamic regret is achievable.

Generalization in Federated Learning: Beyond Training Loss

Generalization Federated Learning

Most convergence analyses of federated algorithms focus on optimization error alone. But in practice, what matters is how well the learned model $\hat{w}$ performs on unseen data. The generalization gap $\mathbb{E}[L(\hat{w})] - L_S(\hat{w})$ depends critically on client count $M$ and local sample size $n$.
